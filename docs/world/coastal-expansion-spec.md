# Spatial world expansion — implementation contract

The homepage world is a compact, playable California coast-to-alpine portfolio. It must
remain a useful route into the conventional site, not become a separate game that hides
the work.

## Product rules

- `View site` always reaches the original fractal navigation at `/atlas`.
- `Explore world` is opt-in. Reduced motion, Save-Data, mobile pre-entry,
  no-JavaScript, and WebGL-failure states preserve ordinary navigation.
- Scenery carries personality. Interface copy stays concise and descriptive.
- The world remains procedural Three.js geometry with no runtime model, texture,
  physics, game iframe, or post-processing download.

## World scale and mountain

Playable bounds are `164 × 154` world units. The north side is now a broad alpine
ridge with multiple overlapping height fields rather than one small hill. The ski
landing is high on the ridge, and the terrain contains:

- a dense but navigable tree run with an authored open lane;
- a ridge kicker shared by ski and bike modes;
- two lower timber ramps for bike/skate transitions;
- fallen redwood/log forms used by both scenery and skateboard grind mechanics;
- distant granite silhouettes that reinforce the ridge without replacing playable
  terrain.

Ski mode stays equipped anywhere in the world. Entering ski mode creates local,
player-following snowfall across the whole explorable world while the physical ridge
still provides the strongest natural snow cover and downhill geometry.

## Equipment and surface contact

Run, skate, bike, ski and boulder remain the five land activity choices.

Bike, skateboard and skis no longer orient from a single terrain sample. Each frame,
`terrainContact` samples front, rear, left and right support points, calculates pitch
and roll, and raises the equipment to the highest sampled support when grounded. This
keeps long equipment above steep terrain instead of letting a wheel, deck or ski slice
through the hillside.

The bike is a lightweight Stumpjumper-inspired trail-bike silhouette: large tires,
visible rims, charcoal frame triangle, gold fork/dropper accents, saddle, bar and
pedal/crank cues. The skateboard has a distinct deck, kick ends, trucks and four wheels.

Ramps and grind logs are defined once in `lib/world/activities.ts`; rendering and
mechanics consume the same authored constants so visual features cannot drift away from
their collision/launch behavior.

## Water, snorkeling and diving

The west coast has a deterministic irregular shoreline, an ocean surface at
`SEA_SURFACE`, and a finite rocky shelf generated by `seaFloorHeight`.

The locomotion state machine is explicit:

`land → surface/snorkel → dive → surface/snorkel → land`

- Walking or jumping into water transitions to surface swimming after contacting the sea.
- Surface movement uses a bounded swim speed and keeps the character at the water line.
- `Dive` begins shallow. `Deeper` and `Shallower` change target depth; `Surface`
  is always available while diving.
- Dive depth is clamped against the local seafloor with a safety clearance and a
  10-unit absolute cap.
- A submerged swimmer cannot tunnel through the shoreline. Returning to shore must
  happen from the surface state.
- Keyboard controls are `V` for dive/surface and `Q/E` for deeper/shallower.
  Equivalent touch buttons remain visible while swimming.
- Shasta waits on the shoreline and never follows the player underwater.

Underwater camera framing, fog and background color are stateful changes to the same
world scene, so surfacing does not load or swap a second environment.

## California kelp reef

The underwater coast is a cold-water kelp and rocky-reef composite, not a tropical
coral reef. It includes procedural giant-kelp forms, rocky relief, schooling fish,
leopard-shark silhouettes, rays, an octopus, an eel, jellies, purple urchins and two
cold-water coral-like formations. Fauna counts are deliberately bounded; repeated
plants, rocks, urchins and coral use instancing to protect draw-call budget.

Field notes link to the National Park Service for terrestrial flora and Monterey Bay
Aquarium references for kelp-forest ecology. The scene is illustrative rather than a
survey of one exact California dive site.

## Arcade cavern

The cavern is a physical game index. A stone back wall renders the current playable
game titles from `content.games` as generated 3×5 carved glyphs, with a few abstract
rock runes around them. Game cards in the discovery panel still use the existing
`/arcade/*` routes. No duplicate game loop runs inside the world renderer.

## Shasta

Shasta uses the existing owner-informed white, tan-gold and reddish-tail palette, but
the mesh now prioritizes recognizable Husky anatomy: athletic deep chest, tapered
muzzle, symmetrical facial mask, upright triangular ears, white socks/paws and a high
curled plume tail. The palette remains art-directed rather than a calibrated physical
fur measurement.

## Acceptance gates

Pure tests cover acceleration/braking, collision, finite bounds, snow landing and
global ski equipment, climb ordering, swimming, shoreline classification, dive depth,
terrain contact, ramp direction and grind-line geometry.

The browser audit covers all seven destinations, all five land modes, bouldering,
mobile navigation, accessibility/fallback behavior, game routes, ecological sources,
renderer diagnostics and the complete land → snorkeling → dive/depth → surface → land
loop. It records desktop/mobile/activity/underwater screenshots for visual inspection.

Production build, typecheck, lint, unit tests and the browser audit are required before
the branch is considered code-qualified. Physical desktop/phone feel and sustained
hardware frame rate remain a separate visual/performance review before merging.


## Stage 3 — alpine, ride and underwater expansion

This revision turns the original small coast-to-hill scene into one continuous
coast-to-alpine activity world. The renderer still uses deterministic procedural
geometry and the existing Three.js dependency; no runtime model, texture, physics,
or postprocessing downloads were added.

### Expanded mountain and skiing

- World bounds now include a broad northern alpine ridge and western ocean shelf.
- The ski landing sits high on the playable ridge, with a winding tree-run lane
  through collidable alpine trunks.
- Selecting Ski keeps skis equipped on land throughout the world instead of silently
  degrading to Run below an arbitrary snowline.
- A bounded particle snowfall follows the active explorer in Ski mode, giving the
  effect of continuous weather without filling the entire GPU scene with particles.
- Shared timber kicker definitions drive both visible ramps and launch mechanics.

### Equipment contact, jumps and grinds

- Bike, skateboard and ski attitude samples front/rear/left/right terrain support.
  Pitch and roll therefore follow the local surface instead of rotating from one
  forward height sample.
- The trail-bike silhouette uses larger wheels, a compact charcoal frame, fork,
  handlebar, saddle/dropper and metallic/gold accents for a recognizable modern
  Stumpjumper-style trail-bike read without loading a branded model.
- The skateboard has separate deck, kicktails, trucks and four wheels.
- Fallen redwood logs are authored once and reused by the renderer and grind
  acquisition logic. Skate action near a log snaps onto the line and exits airborne.
- Bike/skate/ski ramps require compatible mode, speed and approach direction.

### Water, snorkeling and diving

The ocean is part of the same finite coordinate system as land. A deterministic
coastline function separates land from water and a seafloor function defines the
rocky shelf. Entry can happen by walking, riding or jumping from shore.

Aquatic states:

1. **land** — normal equipped activity and jump/climb/grind controls.
2. **surface** — swimming/snorkeling at the waterline; Dive is available.
3. **dive** — underwater swimming with Deeper, Shallower and Surface controls.

Dive depth is clamped to the local seafloor with clearance, so the player cannot
descend through terrain. Surfacing always remains available. Swimming back across
the shoreline returns to land state. Shasta waits on the dry shoreline instead of
following the player underwater. A mask, snorkel and fins appear in aquatic states.

The underwater environment is an imagined California cold-water kelp and rocky-reef
composite. It includes giant kelp, schooling fish, leopard-shark silhouettes, bat
rays, octopus, wolf-eel-like fish, Pacific-sea-nettle-like jellies, purple urchins,
and two stylized coral/sea-fan forms. The stylized coral forms are visual motifs,
not a claim that this is a surveyed shallow tropical coral reef.

### Arcade cavern

The arcade destination now has a rock back wall with procedural stone-carved game
names derived from the live playable-game catalog. Abstract horn/neural/mountain
glyphs flank the lettering. Looking/clicking at the carvings opens the existing
Arcade cavern discovery, where each game links to its established playable route.
No duplicate game engine or iframe is embedded inside the world.

### Shasta

Shasta's procedural model now prioritizes an instantly readable husky silhouette:
athletic deep chest, tapered muzzle, tall triangular ears, facial mask, white socks,
broad paws, saddle/topcoat layers and a high curled plume tail. The same owner-guided
coat palette remains centralized in `SHASTA_COAT`.

### Qualification additions

Unit coverage now includes:

- expanded alpine geometry;
- finite underwater shelf and safe dive clearance;
- swim speed and world bounds;
- persistent Ski selection;
- four-point terrain contact;
- grind acquisition;
- ramp mode/direction/speed gating.

The production browser audit additionally exercises the real land → snorkeling →
dive/deeper → surface → shore loop and asserts Ski-mode snowfall. Existing landmark,
game-link, accessibility, mobile, reduced-motion, no-JavaScript, WebGL-loss and
renderer-budget checks remain release gates.
