# Island Disc Golf v1.2: keyboard-first traversal

**Status:** implemented on \`feat/sids-world\`; needs full CI and physical desktop/mobile playtest. This supersedes \`astra-disc-golf-v1-spec.md\` for user interaction and the course route. No changes to the main world movement, games, biome contract, cave, waterfall, photo archive, or swimming.

## Product intent

The disc golf course should make the player visit different parts of the island and read the terrain, not manipulate a dashboard. The first playtest made precision input the challenge rather than course navigation. Its moving targets, angle slider, aim slider, oscillating power lock, and second accuracy lock created five interacting sources of error. v1.2 removes the moving-target and accuracy-timing demands; wind and trees remain visible, learnable environmental hazards.

## Interaction contract

While disc golf is active, WASD does **not** move the avatar. The player stands at the valid lie, or watches a disc in flight:

| Control | Response |
| --- | --- |
| A/D | Aim left/right in 2° increments relative to the fixed chains |
| W/S | Raise/lower the loft in 2° increments, clamped to 6°..42° |
| Tap Space | Throw at the highlighted suggested power, with centered release |
| Hold Space | Charge a single meter; release to throw with manually chosen power |
| On-screen button | The same tap/hold behavior for pointer/touch and keyboard Enter |
| Numeric hole buttons | Optional course selection, disabled during flight |
| Exit | Restore normal exploration and remove course-only visualization |

A faint flight preview is generated by the same deterministic simulation used by the live throw. The recommendation searches trial powers against real terrain, coastline and wind at the current lie; it is a suggestion, not guaranteed chain capture. It does not claim to predict impact with all placed tree trunks. Feedback is primarily spatial: predicted flight arc, landing ring, distance to fixed chains, light at the actual lie, and visible throw animation. No second release accuracy meter, extra camera mode, or random miss.

## Linked cross-island course

The previous basket location is the next tee. The user can still switch holes explicitly; completing a hole offers a clear next hole action.

| Hole | Tee (x,z) | Fixed basket (x,z) | Par | Design intent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Redwood Traverse | (-6,10) | (42,42) | 4 | Leave the forest for the open island |
| Scrub Crosswind | (42,42) | (43,-29) | 4 | Long exposed crosswind approach |
| Desert Ridge | (43,-29) | (0,-61) | 4 | Terrain and elevation become important |
| Coastal Return | (0,-61) | (-25,15) | 5 | Long return toward the redwoods/coast |

**Survey is required before release.** These endpoints must be inspected in the actual browser for rock or tree overlap, steep slopes, cave roofs, coherent camera framing, and no obstructing discoverables. All targets are inland according to the analytic coastline, which does not prove a physically playable course.

## Flight and fair recovery

- The low end of the speed curve now supports short putts; the previous minimum launch velocity was suitable for a drive, not a two-meter putt.
- A swept proximity check catches discs in the chain volume even between simulation substeps.
- A landed shot outside the catch zone always creates a playable lie; near the basket it prompts an easy Space putt.
- Water and out-of-bounds return to the previous lie without counting a stroke. Tree collision counts a stroke and puts the next lie outside the walker collision radius.
- A recommendation comes from repeated deterministic previews. Accuracy remains precisely centered for tap and hold releases; the player adjusts power and direction intentionally.

## Regression and human signoff

Automated checks: four stationary targets, each distance >45 world units, linked tees, stable wind, power and loft flight sensitivity, swept chain capture, short-putt recommendation, preview/live parity, water refunds, trunk contacts, graphics cleanup. CI must pass typecheck, lint, tests, and production build.

Human checks: first tee and next three transitions; WASD/Space without pointer use; Space held/released under keyboard repeat; click/touch press behavior; at least one realistic putt under 3m; tree/water recoveries; mobile viewport without covering player; target visibility from a distant tee; frame time/resource cleanup; no leakage into ordinary WASD/Space after exit. A green test suite alone does not establish visual or interaction acceptance.
